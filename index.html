The problem I keep coming back to

Relevant is only
the beginning.

In a high-stakes corpus, an answer can sound plausible and still omit the exception that changes what someone should do.

Insurance procedures, policies, and regulatory text contain obligations, permissions, prerequisites, and dependencies across documents. I focus on systems that preserve that structure—and evaluations that detect when they don’t.

A simple example

“Make the policy change” is incomplete when the applicable exception requires specialist approval before execution.

02 / Research trajectory

Follow the failure.
Refine the system.

Three connected lines of empirical work, progressing from familiar baselines to more explicit representations, diagnostics, and execution controls.

From semantic relevance to procedural structure

Neural and symbolic experiments ran in parallel. Explicit procedural representation became a central architectural insight.

  1. 01

    Establish a baseline

    Single-stage sparse–dense retrieval with BM25 and a bi-encoder.

  2. 02

    Diagnose the gap

    Semantic drift audits reveal missing prerequisites, exceptions, and actor–action dependencies.

  3. 03

    Expand the neural path

    Cross-encoder reranking, encoder and LLM adaptation, SPLADE, and ColBERT.

  4. 04

    Make structure explicit

    Neurosymbolic prototypes: knowledge graphs, ontologies, procedure routing, and FSM extraction.

From aggregate scores to procedural validity

Evaluation criteria should assess the answer and supporting evidence independently of the system’s retrieval architecture.

  1. 01

    Measure performance

    Recall@k, nDCG@10, MRR@10, and RAGAS establish initial baselines.

  2. 02

    Ground the references

    SME-developed golden datasets capture applicable rules and expected procedural behavior.

  3. 03

    Diagnose omissions

    Custom harnesses assess constraint recall, entity–action binding, branch conditions, and ordered steps.

  4. 04 / Research

    Formalize the contract

    Compilation fidelity and executable evaluation contracts; planned Z3 SMT constraint solving.

From fixed pipelines to governed agent behavior

Policy optimization and harness governance are complementary workstreams across RAG agents and coding-agent systems.

  1. 01

    Control the pipeline

    Custom Python orchestration with explicit interfaces for retrieval, context, generation, and evaluation.

  2. 02

    Extend the workflow

    Multi-step agents and bounded workflow designs with verification, retry, and escalation controls.

  3. 03

    Optimize the policy

    ART and Unsloth post-training connect custom evals and verifiable rewards to agent behavior.

  4. 04

    Govern execution

    Implemented coding-agent harnesses; research into state-machine orchestration and typed neurosymbolic tools.
